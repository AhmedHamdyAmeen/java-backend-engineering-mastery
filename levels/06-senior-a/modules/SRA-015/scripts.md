# SRA-015 · Cross-functional leadership — Lecture scripts

**Video:** 82 min · **Running case:** *Commerce 2030* — the group formed by Shop and FulfillCo has 25 engineering teams and plans a third-party seller marketplace, a five-country expansion, and doubled order volume each year for three years; the strategy must now be adopted by 25 teams, funded, and executed, with role-players acting as managers, product, finance, security, and executives.
Format: **Goal** · **Key points** · **Show** · **Check**.

---

## SRA-015-L01 · Engineering is one function among several (10 min)
**Goal:** see the business as several functions with different goals, with engineering as one peer.

**Key points**
- Product owns what customers need, finance the money, security and legal the risk, infrastructure the shared platform.
- Each function is judged by its own measures and carries its own constraints.
- Treating partners as blockers makes them behave like blockers.
- Good outcomes need every function; engineering cannot decide alone.

**Show:** a map of the marketplace launch with each function's goal, constraint, and measure of success in a row.

**Check:** Why not treat security as a blocker? *(It has a legitimate goal and constraints; early partnership finds options that a late fight cannot.)*

---

## SRA-015-L02 · What product, finance, and security need from you (12 min)
**Goal:** learn what each partner needs so you can supply it before they ask.

**Key points**
- Product needs options with cost, risk, and date, and early notice of changes.
- Finance needs cost drivers, forecast ranges, and the assumptions behind them.
- Security needs threat context, data flows, and evidence that controls work.
- All need honest risk, not optimistic status.

**Show:** a table with partner, what they need, in what form, and how often: for example, finance receives a cost-per-order forecast with ranges each quarter.

**Check:** What do all partners want from engineering? *(Honest, early information about options, cost, and risk.)*

---

## SRA-015-L03 · Joint planning (10 min)
**Goal:** build plans where engineering trade-offs are visible to partners.

**Key points**
- Plan together from the start, not hand over a finished engineering plan.
- Show the trade-offs as choices: scope, date, cost, risk; each choice has an owner.
- Share assumptions and risks in a single register.
- Agree on checkpoints where the plan will be reviewed.

**Show:** a one-page joint plan: outcome, scope tiers (must, should, could), dates, cost, risks and owners, decision points.

**Check:** Why make trade-offs visible? *(Partners can make informed choices and own the consequences with you.)*

---

## SRA-015-L04 · Negotiating as peers (12 min)
**Goal:** negotiate scope, time, and risk without winning or surrendering.

**Key points**
- Find interests behind positions: "launch on 1 March" may mean "meet the seller commitment".
- Offer options, not ultimatums: reduce scope, move date, add people, accept risk.
- Be explicit about risk you will and will not carry; never hide it to get agreement.
- Record what was agreed and what remains open.

**Show:** a negotiation sheet: their position, their interest, our constraint, three options, our recommendation, the fallback.

**Check:** What do you look for behind a stated demand? *(The interest or goal it serves.)*

---

## SRA-015-L05 · Building relationships across functions (8 min)
**Goal:** build trust before you need it.

**Key points**
- Meet partners regularly outside crises; learn their goals and calendar pressures.
- Share information before they must ask for it, including bad news.
- Keep promises small and kept; trust grows from reliability.
- Credit partners publicly when joint work succeeds.

**Show:** a relationship plan: partner, goal, a standing monthly 30-minute check, what you give, what you hope to learn.

**Check:** When do you build the relationship? *(Before the crisis, through regular contact and honest updates.)*

---

## SRA-015-L06 · Demo: a joint planning session with product and finance (15 min)
**Goal:** run a worked joint planning session for the marketplace launch; prepares SRA-015-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Prepare pre-reads: scope tiers, cost drivers, and three timeline options.
2. Open with the shared outcome: sellers live and orders flowing in the first market.
3. Walk through the scope tiers; finance challenges the cost forecast and assumptions.
4. **Planned mistake:** present one plan and defend it. Fix: present options with trade-offs and let product and finance choose with you.
5. Close with decisions, owners, and the next checkpoint; send the written summary the same day.

---

## SRA-015-L07 · Demo: negotiating a security requirement that threatens a launch date (15 min)
**Goal:** run a worked leadership negotiation with security about a late requirement; prepares SRA-015-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Security requires seller identity verification before payouts; the date is six weeks away.
2. Understand the interest: prevent fraud and meet regulation. Ask what the minimum control is.
3. Develop options: launch with payouts delayed for new sellers, limit payout amounts, or add manual review at first.
4. **Planned mistake:** go to the executive to override security. Fix: agree with security on a phased control with a date for the full one and a named risk owner.
5. Record the decision, the risk accepted, and the review date; update product and finance on the effect.

**Show:** risk acceptance note: control, gap, mitigation, owner, expiry.
