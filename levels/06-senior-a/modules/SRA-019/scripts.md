# SRA-019 · Executive communication — Lecture scripts

**Video:** 80 min · **Running case:** *Commerce 2030* — the group formed by Shop and FulfillCo has 25 engineering teams and plans a third-party seller marketplace, a five-country expansion, and doubled order volume each year for three years; the strategy must now be adopted by 25 teams, funded, and executed, with role-players acting as managers, product, finance, security, and executives.
Format: **Goal** · **Key points** · **Show** · **Check**.

---

## SRA-019-L01 · How executives decide (10 min)
**Goal:** understand what executives weigh and the time they have.

**Key points**
- They decide across many topics with little time; they want the answer first and detail on request.
- They weigh revenue, customers, regulators, cost, and risk to the company.
- They trust people who are clear, honest about uncertainty, and specific about what they need.
- A decision is a request with a deadline, an owner, and consequences of delay.

**Show:** a table of what an executive asks of any proposal: what do you want from me, why now, what happens if we do nothing, what does it cost, how sure are you?

**Check:** What do executives want first? *(The recommendation and what you need from them.)*

---

## SRA-019-L02 · From technical risk to business impact (12 min)
**Goal:** translate technical findings into effects an executive can weigh.

**Key points**
- Replace technical terms with outcomes: lost orders, delayed launch, regulatory fine, higher cost.
- Quantify with ranges and state the assumptions; a rough number beats an adjective.
- Connect to something the business already tracks (revenue per hour, seller churn).
- Show the cost of doing nothing next to the cost of acting.

**Show:** translation: "single database failover untested" becomes "a database failure during peak could stop checkout for up to two hours, about X orders lost per hour (range, assumption)".

**Check:** How do you make a risk understandable to a CEO? *(Express it as business impact with a range and the assumptions behind it.)*

---

## SRA-019-L03 · The executive brief (10 min)
**Goal:** write a one-page brief with a fixed structure.

**Key points**
- Structure: situation, recommendation, options, ask.
- Situation: two or three sentences with the key number.
- Options: two or three with cost, risk, and effect; show why the recommendation wins.
- Ask: one specific decision, amount, and date.

**Show:** brief outline: situation (three lines), recommendation (one line), options table, risks and assumptions, ask with decision date.

**Check:** What ends the brief? *(A specific ask: the decision, amount, and date.)*

---

## SRA-019-L04 · Handling hard questions (10 min)
**Goal:** answer difficult questions briefly and honestly.

**Key points**
- Answer first in one sentence, then give the reason; stop.
- Prepare the ten hardest questions beforehand: cost, delay, blame, alternatives.
- If you do not know, say so, say how you will find out, and by when.
- Do not overstate certainty; give a range and what would change it.

**Show:** hard-question card: question, one-sentence answer, supporting fact, what we do not know, when we will know.

**Check:** What do you do if you do not know the answer? *(Say so, and give how and when you will find out.)*

---

## SRA-019-L05 · Getting to a decision (8 min)
**Goal:** leave the room with a decision inside the time slot.

**Key points**
- Pre-wire: talk to key executives beforehand so there are no surprises.
- Open with the ask and the time you need; time-box discussion.
- When discussion drifts, restate the options and the decision needed.
- Close by confirming the decision, owner, and next steps in writing the same day.

**Show:** a ten-minute pitch timing: ask and situation (2), options and recommendation (3), risks (2), discussion and decision (3).

**Check:** Why pre-wire executives? *(To resolve objections privately so the meeting can decide.)*

---

## SRA-019-L06 · Demo: a 10-minute funding pitch for the modernization program (15 min)
**Goal:** build and deliver a funding pitch for year one of Commerce 2030; prepares SRA-019-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Write the ask: funding for year one, with the outcome it buys (marketplace-ready platform, fewer outages).
2. Translate risks into business impact with ranges and assumptions.
3. Build a one-page backup: options, cost, benefits, risks.
4. **Planned mistake:** open with the architecture. Fix: open with the situation and the ask; keep architecture to one backup slide.
5. Rehearse the hard questions (why now, why this much, what if we pay less) and run the pitch against the clock.

---

## SRA-019-L07 · Demo: briefing the CEO after a major outage (15 min)
**Goal:** run a worked briefing after a four-hour checkout outage; prepares SRA-019-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Prepare a five-minute brief: what happened, customer and revenue impact, current state, cause so far, what we do next.
2. State what is known and what is not, and when the next update will arrive.
3. Offer two or three actions with cost and effect, and the one you recommend.
4. **Planned mistake:** defend the team or explain the root cause in technical detail. Fix: lead with impact and recovery, acknowledge the failure plainly, and keep cause at one sentence.
5. Close with the decision needed (for example, a resilience investment) and follow up in writing within a day.

**Show:** brief outline: impact, status, cause so far, actions, ask, next update time.
