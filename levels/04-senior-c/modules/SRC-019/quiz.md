# SRC-019 · Incident leadership — Quiz

Outcomes: O1 incident commander · O2 stakeholder communication · O3 decisions under uncertainty · O4 postmortems.

**Q01 (O1)** The incident commander's main job is to: A. Write all the code B. Coordinate people, priorities, and decisions C. Fix the bug personally D. Talk to no one
**Q02 (O1)** Why not debug as IC? A. It is forbidden by law B. It is boring C. It costs money D. It removes the overall view
**Q03 (O1)** During an incident, first priority is usually: A. Root cause analysis B. Writing the postmortem C. Mitigating impact D. Finding who is at fault
**Q04 (O2)** Every stakeholder update should include: A. The next update time B. A guess of root cause C. Blame D. Nothing if no change
**Q05 (O2)** When nothing has changed, you should: A. Stay silent B. Send an update saying so with the next update time C. Wait for resolution D. Close the incident
**Q06 (O3)** Under uncertainty, prefer options that are: A. Irreversible and fast B. Untested C. Reversible with limited blast radius D. Complex
**Q07 (O3)** A risky decision should be recorded with: A. Nothing B. Reasoning, assumption, and check C. Only the result D. A signature
**Q08 (O4)** A blameless postmortem asks: A. Who caused it? B. Who should be fired? C. Why was it not hidden? D. What made the action seem reasonable and what factors contributed?
**Q09 (O4)** Good postmortem actions are: A. Many and vague B. Few, specific, owned, and dated C. Anonymous D. Optional
**Q10 (O4)** A postmortem finding a single "root cause" is: A. Preferred B. Required C. Often too narrow; complex failures have several contributing factors D. Impossible

## Answers
Q01 B · Q02 D · Q03 C · Q04 A · Q05 B · Q06 C · Q07 B · Q08 D · Q09 B · Q10 C

## Practical part
Incident-command simulation (SRC-019 assessment, graduation gate component) and the `SRC-019-LAB1` postmortem. Pass: at least 8/10 on the quiz, and a simulation assessed as sound on coordination, communication, and action quality.
