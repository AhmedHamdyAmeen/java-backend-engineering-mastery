# STF-015 · Mentoring at scale — Lecture scripts

**Video:** 94 min · **Running case:** *Platform 2.0* — the strategy, governance, and platform direction from STF-P1 must now be adopted by about 20 product teams who did not ask for them; role-players act as engineering managers, skeptical tech leads, product, finance, security, and executives.
Format: **Goal** · **Key points** · **Show** · **Check**.

---

## STF-015-L01 · From mentoring people to growing an organization (10 min)
**Goal:** shift from helping individuals to building capability across teams.

**Key points**
- One-to-one time does not scale; you have a few hours a week.
- Leverage: grow people who grow others, build communities, write things once.
- Pick where your effort changes the most: seniors close to Staff scope, practices that spread.
- Measure by the scope others take on, not by hours spent.

**Show:** a leverage table: one-to-one mentoring (1 person), a community (30 people), a written guide (everyone), with effort and effect for each.

**Check:** Why does one-to-one time not scale? *(Your hours are fixed, and the need is larger.)*

---

## STF-015-L02 · Mentoring vs sponsorship (12 min)
**Goal:** tell advice from advocacy, and do both.

**Key points**
- A mentor gives advice and feedback; a sponsor uses their reputation to open opportunities.
- Sponsorship means naming the person in rooms they are not in and putting their name on visible work.
- It carries risk for the sponsor; sponsor only people whose work you can vouch for.
- Be explicit with the person about what you are doing.

**Show:** a two-column table: mentor actions (review a design, give feedback) versus sponsor actions (recommend for the platform working group, give them the stage at the architecture forum).

**Check:** What does a sponsor do that a mentor may not? *(Advocates for the person and creates opportunities using their own reputation.)*

---

## STF-015-L03 · Stretch assignments (10 min)
**Goal:** give work that grows scope safely.

**Key points**
- Stretch work is slightly beyond current scope, with real stakes and a safety net.
- Examples: lead a cross-team RFC, run a migration wave, present to executives.
- Agree goals, check-ins, and what support you give; do not take it back at the first problem.
- Make scope visible: the person's name on the outcome.

**Show:** a stretch assignment brief: assignment, why it fits, success criteria, support, check-in dates, visibility.

**Check:** What makes a stretch safe? *(Clear goals, check-ins, and support, with you stepping in only on real risk.)*

---

## STF-015-L04 · Technical communities that last (12 min)
**Goal:** build communities of practice that survive past launch.

**Key points**
- A community needs a purpose, a regular rhythm, a few active leaders, and visible output.
- Start with a real shared problem (for example Platform 2.0 adoption), not a topic list.
- Rotate leadership so it does not depend on you.
- Retire communities that no longer serve a purpose.

**Show:** a community charter: purpose, members, rhythm, leaders, outputs, how success is judged, review date.

**Check:** What kills most communities? *(No clear purpose and no one owning the rhythm.)*

---

## STF-015-L05 · Scaling knowledge: talks, docs, office hours (10 min)
**Goal:** spread knowledge beyond one-to-one conversations.

**Key points**
- Write answers once: short guides, decision records, example repositories.
- Talks and recordings for common topics; office hours for specific problems.
- Track repeated questions; they show what to document.
- Keep materials current; outdated docs lose trust.

**Show:** a plan: top ten questions, which become a doc, a talk, or an office-hour topic, with owner and refresh date.

**Check:** How do you decide what to document? *(Questions that are asked repeatedly.)*

---

## STF-015-L06 · Inclusive sponsorship (10 min)
**Goal:** widen who gets sponsored.

**Key points**
- Sponsorship goes by default to people like us; notice and correct.
- Look at who gets visible work, and who is missing from your network.
- Recognize different paths: bootcamp, non-traditional, remote, part-time.
- Use criteria based on work and potential, not familiarity.

**Show:** an audit: last year's stretch assignments by person and background, with gaps highlighted and three actions.

**Check:** Why audit who you sponsor? *(To see and correct bias toward familiar people.)*

---

## STF-015-L07 · Demo: a sponsorship conversation with a senior engineer (15 min)
**Goal:** run a conversation that agrees a stretch path to Staff scope; prepares STF-015-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Prepare: read their recent work, note evidence of scope, pick a stretch candidate.
2. Ask about their goals before offering ideas.
3. Name specific strengths and the one gap that matters for Staff scope.
4. **Planned mistake:** giving only advice ("you should be more visible"). Fix: offer a concrete assignment and say you will advocate for it.
5. Agree success criteria, check-ins, and visibility.
6. Write a short follow-up note and calendar the first check-in.

---

## STF-015-L08 · Demo: relaunching a dying architecture community (15 min)
**Goal:** revive a community by finding a purpose; prepares STF-015-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Diagnose: attendance falling, same two speakers, no decisions.
2. Interview ten members: what would make it worth their time?
3. Reset the purpose around Platform 2.0 adoption problems.
4. **Planned mistake:** relaunching with a bigger kickoff and the same format. Fix: change the format first: shorter, problem-driven, rotating hosts.
5. Name three rotating leaders and a visible output each quarter.
6. Set a review date and agree what happens if it still does not work.
