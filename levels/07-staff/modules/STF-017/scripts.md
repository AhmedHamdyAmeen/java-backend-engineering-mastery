# STF-017 · Executive communication — Lecture scripts

**Video:** 88 min · **Running case:** *Platform 2.0* — the strategy, governance, and platform direction from STF-P1 must now be adopted by about 20 product teams who did not ask for them; role-players act as engineering managers, skeptical tech leads, product, finance, security, and executives.
Format: **Goal** · **Key points** · **Show** · **Check**.

---

## STF-017-L01 · What executives need from Staff engineers (12 min)
**Goal:** understand how executives decide and what they want to hear.

**Key points**
- Executives compare many investments with limited time and money.
- They need: the decision, the business effect, cost, risk, and when they will see results.
- They distrust jargon, surprises, and certainty about uncertain things.
- They also want early warning, not only pitches.

**Show:** a one-slide frame: decision needed, why now, cost, benefit, risk, what happens if we do nothing.

**Check:** What does an executive need first? *(The decision requested and why it matters to the business.)*

---

## STF-017-L02 · The engineering business case (14 min)
**Goal:** build a business case with costs, benefits, and risks that can be checked later.

**Key points**
- Costs: people, infrastructure, licenses, migration, opportunity cost.
- Benefits: time saved, incidents avoided, revenue enabled, risk reduced; convert to money with stated assumptions.
- Show a range (low, expected, high) and what must be true.
- Define how and when benefits will be measured.

**Show:** a cost model sheet: inputs, assumptions, three scenarios, payback period, and review dates (all figures are illustrative; verify before recording).

**Check:** Why show a range rather than one number? *(The future is uncertain; ranges are more honest and more credible.)*

---

## STF-017-L03 · Cost and risk narratives (12 min)
**Goal:** present cost and risk so they compare with other investments.

**Key points**
- Use the same units finance uses: cost per year, cost per service, loss avoided.
- Describe risk as likelihood and impact in business terms (outage hours, audit findings).
- Compare with alternatives and with doing nothing.
- Be explicit about the risks of the investment itself.

**Show:** a comparison table: Platform 2.0 versus "extend current pipelines" and "do nothing", on cost, risk reduction, time to value.

**Check:** Why describe risk in business terms? *(Executives weigh it against other business risks.)*

---

## STF-017-L04 · Technical storytelling (12 min)
**Goal:** tell the story of a technical initiative in business terms.

**Key points**
- Structure: situation, problem, decision, result, next step.
- Use one concrete example (a three-week delay to launch a service) instead of abstractions.
- Describe in the executive's language; keep technical detail in backup.
- Avoid technology-first stories; start with the customer or business effect.

**Show:** a three-minute story outline: "Last quarter a new payments feature waited three weeks for pipeline setup; this is what it cost, and what changes."

**Check:** What should a story start with? *(The business situation or problem, not the technology.)*

---

## STF-017-L05 · Ongoing executive relationships (8 min)
**Goal:** maintain relationships beyond one-off pitches.

**Key points**
- Short regular updates, with good and bad news.
- Learn their goals and constraints; connect your work to them.
- Ask for advice and feedback, not only decisions.
- Follow up on benefits you promised; credibility compounds.

**Show:** a quarterly executive update template: progress, metrics versus plan, risks, one decision or ask.

**Check:** Why report bad news early? *(It builds trust and gives them time to help.)*

---

## STF-017-L06 · Demo: the Platform 2.0 business case for the board (15 min)
**Goal:** build and rehearse a 15-minute presentation; prepares STF-017-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Write the decision and the one-sentence reason first.
2. Build the cost model with finance's units and a three-scenario range.
3. Prepare the story, comparison table, and risk slide; move technical detail to backup.
4. **Planned mistake:** opening with the architecture. Fix: open with the cost of the current state and the decision requested.
5. Rehearse with role-played CEO, CFO, and CTO; note their hard questions.
6. Prepare answers and the follow-up measures.

---

## STF-017-L07 · Demo: explaining a year of platform spend to the CFO (15 min)
**Goal:** present actual spend against the business case; prepares STF-017-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Gather the year's actual costs by category and compare with the plan.
2. Explain variances plainly: what was higher, why, and what changed.
3. Show benefits realized versus promised, with data sources.
4. **Planned mistake:** hiding an overspend in a total. Fix: show it separately with cause and corrective action.
5. Let finance reproduce the numbers from your sheet.
6. Agree the next-year budget and reporting.
