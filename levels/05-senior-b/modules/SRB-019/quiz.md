# SRB-019 · Risk management — Quiz

Outcomes: O1 identifying risks · O2 risk registers · O3 reducing uncertainty early · O4 contingency planning.

**Q01 (O1)** A risk that has already happened is called: A. A trigger B. A spike C. A contingency D. An issue
**Q02 (O1)** A pre-mortem asks: A. How did we succeed? B. Who is to blame? C. Imagine the program failed; what caused it? D. When will it end?
**Q03 (O1)** Which is a dependency risk? A. A key engineer is on leave B. Another team's API may arrive late C. An estimate has no slack D. New technology is unproven
**Q04 (O2)** Every register entry needs: A. A color B. A long description C. A ticket D. A named owner and an observable trigger
**Q05 (O2)** A register becomes useful when: A. It is large B. It is written once C. Reviews on a rhythm change plans and priorities D. It is hidden
**Q06 (O3)** A spike is: A. A time-boxed investigation to answer a technical question B. A production release C. A budget increase D. A risk owner
**Q07 (O3)** Why tackle the riskiest part first? A. It is easier B. Learning early leaves time to change the plan C. It looks good D. Managers prefer it
**Q08 (O3)** Staged commitments mean: A. Committing to everything now B. Splitting teams C. Committing to the next stage until risks fall D. Delaying forever
**Q09 (O4)** Mitigation versus contingency: A. They are the same B. Contingency acts before the risk happens C. Mitigation is only for people risks D. Mitigation acts beforehand; contingency is the plan after the risk occurs
**Q10 (O4)** Why define the invocation threshold in advance? A. So the decision follows the plan, not hope or pressure B. To avoid communication C. To save cost D. To skip the register

## Answers
Q01 D · Q02 C · Q03 B · Q04 D · Q05 C · Q06 A · Q07 B · Q08 C · Q09 D · Q10 A

## Practical part
Complete the risk-response simulation (`SRB-019-LAB1`, SRB-019 assessment), where the assessor triggers one risk. Pass: at least 8/10 on the quiz, a register with owners and triggers, and a response that follows the contingency plan or justifies the deviation.
