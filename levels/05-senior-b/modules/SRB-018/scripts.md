# SRB-018 · Mentoring senior peers — Lecture scripts

**Video:** 82 min · **Running case:** *The FulfillCo integration* — the engineer leads the four-team program planned in SRB-P1 through delivery; role-players act as team leads, product, finance, and security.
Format: **Goal** · **Key points** · **Show** · **Check**.

---

## SRB-018-L01 · Mentoring people at your own level (10 min)
**Goal:** describe how mentoring changes when the other person is as senior as you.

**Key points**
- Peers do not need teaching; they need a sounding board, different perspective, and honest feedback.
- Ask questions that surface assumptions rather than giving answers.
- Respect their ownership: you help them decide, you do not decide for them.
- Build trust first: show your own uncertainty and learn from them in return.

**Show** A comparison table: mentoring a junior (teach, guide steps) versus a senior peer (question, challenge, share options).

**Check:** What is the main difference when mentoring a peer? *(You help them think and decide rather than telling them what to do.)*

---

## SRB-018-L02 · Facilitating design discussions (12 min)
**Goal:** run a discussion where the best idea wins, not the loudest voice.

**Key points**
- Clarify the decision and the criteria first.
- Get written positions before the discussion so dominant voices do not anchor others.
- Give each person time; ask quieter participants directly.
- Restate each position fairly before challenging it.
- Close with a decision, owner, and next step, or a dated experiment that will decide.

**Show** A facilitation outline: frame (5 min), written positions (5), steelman each option (10), criteria scoring (10), decision or experiment (5).

**Check:** Why collect written positions first? *(It prevents the first or loudest voice from anchoring the group.)*

---

## SRB-018-L03 · Technical conflict resolution (12 min)
**Goal:** resolve disagreements between senior engineers with structure.

**Key points**
- Find what the disagreement is about: facts, values, or priorities.
- Turn opinions into testable claims; agree an experiment or a data source.
- Separate positions from interests: what does each person fear will go wrong?
- Use decision techniques: criteria scoring, time-boxed spike, reversible-first choice, or a named decider.
- Agree "disagree and commit": once decided, everyone supports it and the dissent is recorded.

**Show** Conflict worksheet: claim A | claim B | underlying concern | evidence to gather | decider | review date.

**Check:** What does "disagree and commit" require? *(A clear decision, recorded dissent, and real support for the outcome.)*

---

## SRB-018-L04 · Influence through understanding (10 min)
**Goal:** influence decisions without formal authority.

**Key points**
- Learn what others care about before arguing your case.
- Build shared understanding with a short written problem statement and data.
- Involve people early, so the proposal becomes shared.
- Use small wins and reversible experiments to build credibility.
- Accept that you may be wrong, and say so.

**Show** An influence map: stakeholder | what they care about | their concern | what would help | who they trust.

**Check:** What comes before presenting your solution when you lack authority? *(Understanding what others care about and building shared understanding of the problem.)*

---

## SRB-018-L05 · Peer feedback (8 min)
**Goal:** give feedback a senior peer can act on.

**Key points**
- Be specific: describe the observed behavior and its effect, not a judgment of character.
- Ask permission and choose a private setting.
- Check your understanding by asking for their view first.
- Agree one concrete next step.
- Feedback goes both ways; ask for it yourself.

**Show** A feedback template: *in situation X, I saw Y, the effect was Z; what is your view? what might you try?*

**Check:** What makes feedback actionable? *(It names specific behavior and effect, and ends with a concrete next step.)*

---

## SRB-018-L06 · Demo: facilitating a deadlocked design debate (15 min)
**Goal:** facilitate two senior engineers split on the stock-ledger design to a decision both commit to; prepares SRB-018-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Meet each engineer separately to hear their position and underlying concern.
2. Open the joint session with the decision, the date, and criteria (correctness, operability, delivery date, reversibility).
3. Ask each to restate the other's position until the other agrees it is fair.
4. **Planned mistake:** offer your own preferred design to break the tie. Fix: return to the criteria and agree a short experiment or a named decider.
5. Score options against criteria; turn the disputed claim into a test.
6. Record the decision, the dissent, and the review date; confirm both commit.

**Check:** Why restate the other's position? *(It shows you understand it and often reveals shared ground.)*

---

## SRB-018-L07 · Demo: giving hard feedback to a senior peer (15 min)
**Goal:** give difficult feedback on a peer's dismissive behavior in design reviews; prepares SRB-018-LAB1.

**Steps**
1. Collect two or three specific examples with dates and effects.
2. Ask for a private conversation and state the purpose in one sentence.
3. Ask for their perspective first, and listen.
4. **Planned mistake:** use labels such as "arrogant". Fix: describe observed behavior and its effect.
5. Agree one concrete change and a check-in date.
6. Follow up with specific recognition when you see the change.

**Check:** Why start by asking their view? *(They may have context you lack, and it lowers defensiveness.)*
